## Reviewer claims judged unconvincing or not acted upon

- **R-Fit §4** ("a modern biostatistician would simply fit a Bayesian hierarchical state-space model"): true but orthogonal — the question is about *exact* rule-defined events, not prediction. No change beyond existing Bortot et al. reference.
- **R-Lit "Drikvandi et al. 2013, Biometrical Journal 55(4)"**: bibliographic details could not be reconciled with known work (Drikvandi et al. 2013 is in *Biostatistics*; the permutation LMM random-effects test is Lee & Braun 2012, *Biometrics*). Not cited as given.
- **R-Lit "Apple Women's Health Study 2023"**: relevant but bibliographic details uncertain offline; not added (flagged).
- **R-Stat B3 "compare against max-T"**: a qualitative comparison is already in §5.3; a simulation comparison of joint statistics would be a methods paper's job, inconsistent with Case Study framing. Not added.

## Positioning decision (Stage 3)

**Case Study.** Reasons: (i) the quadratic-form permutation statistic is an adaptation (PERMANOVA-type / energy-distance lineage) and the manuscript itself deferred theory to future work; (ii) BJ guidance treats one-dataset analyses without methodological advance as unsuitable for Research Articles but accepts Case Studies with novel analytical aspects; (iii) the genuinely reusable insight is not the statistic but the *contrast between pooled, i.i.d. and within-subject conditional nulls for rule-defined pattern counts in clustered longitudinal sequences* — the same data yield p < .001 or p ≈ .12 depending on which exchangeability is assumed, and the paper shows why. That lesson is stated once, concretely, and not inflated.
