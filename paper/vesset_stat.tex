% ─── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\newpage
\begin{center}{\bfseries Abstract}\end{center}

\noindent
Jewish religious law (\textit{Halacha}) prescribes that women anticipate menstruation
based on recurring patterns in their menstrual cycle history --- rules collectively
known as \textit{vesset} (Hebrew: anticipated pattern of menstrual onset). Of the 17 vesset pattern types documented in halachic
literature, 7 are testable with cycle-length data alone; of these, 5 have positive
observed frequencies and are analysed here: \textit{Haflaga} (fixed interval),
\textit{Dilug} (arithmetic progression), \textit{Week} (weekly anchor),
\textit{Week-Dilug} (30-day anchor), and \textit{Dilug-in-Dilug} (second-order
progression). The five patterns form a polynomial hierarchy (degrees 0, 0, 0, 1, 2)
whose structure generates mathematically necessary dependencies between them.
We apply permutation testing and a multinomial sampling null model --- introduced in
\cite{ross2022phd} and inspired by the simulation-based framework of King et al.\
\cite{king2018} --- to a dataset of 1,554 menstrual cycles from 118 women
\cite{fehring2013}. Pairwise chi-square tests confirm significant positive dependence
between Haflaga, Week-Dilug, and Dilug-in-Dilug ($p < .01$ for both pairs), which we
derive mathematically from set-theoretic inclusion and proximity in the polynomial
hierarchy. Results reveal that \textit{Haflaga} ($z = 4.51$, $p < .001$) and
\textit{Dilug} ($z = 1.83$, $p = .043$) are significantly overrepresented under both
null models. Under the permutation null, only 2 of 50,000 simulations reached or
exceeded the observed Haflaga count of 26, which ties the simulation maximum. A joint
multivariate test \cite{ross2022phd} --- based on the Euclidean distance between the
observed count vector and the null mean vector, accommodating the dependence structure
without assuming independence --- is highly significant under both models ($p < .01$).
A joint test restricted to the three mathematically dependent patterns (Haflaga, Week-Dilug, Dilug-in-Dilug) is also significant ($p < .01$). These findings provide the first rigorous statistical
validation that two core halachic vesset rules reflect genuine regularities in
menstrual cycle data.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} menstrual cycle, vesset, Halacha, permutation test,
multinomial sampling, natural family planning, Jewish law, polynomial hierarchy,
dependence structure, multivariate randomization test

% ─── 1. Introduction ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\newpage
\section{Introduction}

Menstrual cycles exhibit substantial variability both within and across individuals
\cite{munster1992,creinin2004,symul2019}. The cycle length distribution is
approximately bell-shaped with a pronounced right tail (skewness $\approx 1.3$ in the
present dataset), reflecting a small proportion of unusually long cycles. Within-person
consistency is moderate: intraclass correlation coefficients for cycle length are
typically in the range of $0.3$--$0.5$ \cite{dasharathy2012}, indicating meaningful
regularity beneath the surface variability. Bayesian hierarchical state-space models have
been proposed to exploit this temporal structure for cycle-length prediction
\cite{bortot2010}, confirming that within-woman dynamics carry genuine predictive
information.

In the Jewish religious tradition, this regularity is also subject to detailed legal
analysis. Halachic law (\textit{Halacha}, Jewish religious law) requires that a woman anticipate the onset of
her next menstruation based on patterns discernible in her prior cycle history. These
anticipatory rules are called \textit{vesset}, and they govern a system of abstinence
obligations central to the observance of \textit{taharat hamishpacha} (family purity: the traditional Jewish
system of laws governing marital intimacy in relation to the menstrual cycle). Of the 17 vesset pattern types documented in halachic literature, 10 require
date or time-of-day data unavailable in standard cycle-length records; the remaining
7 are testable empirically. Two of these 7 (Dilug Chozer Chalila and Haflaga Chozer Chalila
(Hebrew: repeating-cycle arithmetic progression and repeating fixed interval,
respectively) occurred zero times in the present dataset and are not reported separately.
The five patterns with positive observed frequencies are analysed here.

These five patterns form a \textit{polynomial hierarchy}: each describes a \textit{haflaga} (Hebrew: inter-menstrual interval, $H_k = L_k + 1$)
sequence that follows a polynomial of a specific degree in cycle index. Haflaga,
Week, and Week-Dilug are all degree-0 (constant) sequences; Dilug is degree-1
(linear); Dilug-in-Dilug is degree-2 (quadratic). This hierarchy generates
mathematically necessary dependence relationships between the patterns, with
implications for how their joint frequencies should be tested.

Despite the centuries-long practical application of vesset rules --- and the modern
availability of large longitudinal cycle datasets --- no published study has formally
tested whether these patterns occur in empirical data at rates exceeding chance. This
is a non-trivial question: if vesset rules merely detect random fluctuations, their
anticipatory validity is weakened. Conversely, if they are significantly
overrepresented, their empirical basis is independently corroborated.

The present study addresses this gap by applying two complementary null models ---
a permutation test \cite{edgington2007} and a multinomial sampling null
\cite{ross2022phd} --- to a longitudinal NFP dataset \cite{fehring2013}. We derive
per-pattern p-values, effect sizes, and a joint multivariate test that accommodates
the dependence structure among pattern counts without assuming independence. The joint
test is an original methodological contribution \cite{ross2022phd}, inspired by the
simulation-based testing philosophy of King et al.\ \cite{king2018} though distinct
from their density-based approach; a formal theoretical treatment is planned in future
work. We also characterise the mathematical dependence structure through pairwise
chi-square tests and derive the dependencies analytically from the pattern definitions.

This study extends a line of quantitative halachic scholarship \cite{ross2022markov,
ross2019reliability,ross2021statistical}. To our knowledge it is the first to test
vesset pattern frequencies against an explicit null model using modern hypothesis
testing.

% ─── 2. Background ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Background}

\subsection{The Polynomial Hierarchy of Vesset Rules}

Let $L_1, L_2, \ldots, L_n$ denote the menstrual cycle lengths (in days) of a single
woman, and define the \textit{haflaga} $H_k = L_k + 1$ (the interval from the first
day of menstruation $k$ to the first day of menstruation $k+1$, following halachic
convention \cite{ross2021statistical}). The five vesset pattern types are:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textit{Week-Dilug.}] $H_k = H_{k-1} = 30$ (two or more consecutive haflagot
    both equal to 30). Degree-0 polynomial (constant) at the canonical 30-day anchor.
    Minimum run: 2 cycles.

  \item[\textit{Week.}] $H_k = H_{k-1}$, $H_k \in \mathcal{W}$ where
    $\mathcal{W} = \{H_{\min}+3, H_{\min}+10, \ldots\}$ (values spaced 7 apart).
    Degree-0 polynomial anchored to weekly values. Minimum run: 2 cycles.

  \item[\textit{Haflaga.}] $H_k = H_{k-1} = H_{k-2}$ (three or more consecutive
    equal haflagot, any value). Degree-0 polynomial (constant, unrestricted).
    Minimum run: 3 cycles.

  \item[\textit{Dilug.}] $H_k - H_{k-1} = H_{k-1} - H_{k-2} = d \neq 0$ (constant
    non-zero first difference). Degree-1 polynomial (linear).
    Minimum run: 3 cycles.

  \item[\textit{Dilug-in-Dilug.}] $(H_k - H_{k-1}) - (H_{k-1} - H_{k-2}) =
    (H_{k-1} - H_{k-2}) - (H_{k-2} - H_{k-3}) = d \neq 0$ (constant non-zero second
    difference). Degree-2 polynomial (quadratic). Minimum run: 4 cycles.
\end{description}

The hierarchy Haflaga (degree 0) $\to$ Dilug (degree 1) $\to$ Dilug-in-Dilug
(degree 2) mirrors the classical Taylor expansion: a constant function is the
zeroth-order approximation, a linear function the first-order, and a quadratic the
second-order.

\subsection{Mathematical Dependence Structure}

The polynomial hierarchy generates three analytically derivable dependence
relationships, which we verify empirically in Section~\ref{sec:chisq}:

\paragraph{Haflaga and Week-Dilug --- set-theoretic inclusion.}
Week-Dilug requires $H_k = H_{k-1} = 30$; Haflaga-30 requires $H_k = H_{k-1} =
H_{k-2} = 30$. Therefore:
\[
  \{\text{Haflaga-30 sequence}\} \;\subseteq\; \{\text{Week-Dilug sequence}\}.
\]
Every Haflaga sequence with value 30 automatically generates a Week-Dilug event in
its first two cycles. This is set-theoretic inclusion, not merely statistical
correlation. In the present data, 3 of the 26 observed Haflaga sequences have value
$H = 30$, each contributing directly to the Week-Dilug count of 18.

\paragraph{Haflaga and Dilug-in-Dilug --- proximity in the hierarchy.}
When the second difference $|d|$ of a Dilug-in-Dilug sequence is small, its first
differences are nearly equal and the haflaga sequence is nearly constant --- i.e.\
nearly a Haflaga. In the data, 16 of 27 observed DiD sequences have $|d| = 1$,
making them almost indistinguishable from a constant sequence. Both patterns further
share the same structural prerequisite: a minimum run of $\geq 3$ consecutive cycles
satisfying a regularity condition, selecting the same women with high within-person
cycle stability (within-person SD: Haflaga women $= 1.54$ days vs.\ no-vesset women
$= 3.17$ days).

\paragraph{Dilug and Week-Dilug --- logical incompatibility.}
Dilug requires $\Delta H_k \neq 0$ (strictly non-constant differences); Week-Dilug
requires $H_k = H_{k-1} = 30$ ($\Delta H = 0$). These conditions are mutually
exclusive within the same cycle window, creating a negative dependence tendency.

\subsection{Prior Work}

\paragraph{Menstrual cycle statistics.}
Variability and regularity of cycle length have been extensively characterised
\cite{munster1992,creinin2004,dasharathy2012,symul2019}. Bortot et al.\
\cite{bortot2010} developed a Bayesian hierarchical state-space model for sequential
prediction of cycle lengths, exploiting within-woman temporal dependence; their model
provides a benchmark for cycle-length predictability.

\paragraph{Quantitative halachic analysis.}
Ross \cite{ross2022markov} modelled cycle transitions with Markov chains;
\cite{ross2019reliability} evaluated reliability of self-reported menstrual data in
a halachic context; \cite{ross2021statistical} applied statistical analysis to
abstinence laws. The present study adds the first formal hypothesis test of whether
vesset patterns are overrepresented in real data.

\paragraph{Randomization-based testing.}
Permutation tests \cite{edgington2007,good2005} provide exact finite-sample p-values
under exchangeability. The multinomial sampling null \cite{ross2022phd} draws each
woman's cycles independently from the fitted empirical distribution, directly encoding
the null hypothesis of cycle independence. For the joint test, King et al.\
\cite{king2018} proposed a general framework for testing a vector of statistics via
simulation of the joint density, using a multivariate kernel density estimator to
define a smallest-acceptance-region test. The present joint test differs: we use the
Euclidean distance from the null mean vector as the test statistic, which is simpler,
requires no density estimation, and has an intuitive geometric interpretation. A formal
theoretical comparison and analysis of the distance-based approach is planned in future
work \cite{ross2022phd}.

% ─── 3. Methods ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset}

We analyzed a publicly available longitudinal dataset originally collected by Fehring
et al.\ \cite{fehring2013} in a randomised trial comparing two internet-supported
natural family planning methods, archived at Marquette University
\cite{marquette2013data}. After excluding cycles shorter than 18 days or longer than
54 days \cite{creinin2004}, the final sample comprised \textbf{1,554 cycles from 118
women} ($M = 13.2$ cycles per woman, $\text{SD} = 7.0$, range $1$--$45$).

Raw cycle lengths ranged from 18 to 54 days ($M = 29.3$, $\text{SD} = 3.9$,
$\text{Mdn} = 28.5$, skewness $= 1.29$). The distribution is approximately
bell-shaped with a right tail, consistent with the literature
\cite{munster1992,creinin2004}. Women ranged in age from 21 to 43 years
($M = 30.6$, $\text{SD} = 5.7$). Two charting cohorts were represented
($n_0 = 96$, $n_1 = 67$). All cycle lengths were incremented by 1 to conform to the
halachic haflaga convention ($H_k = L_k + 1$), yielding $M = 30.3$, $\text{SD} = 3.9$.

The within-person variability structure is relevant to the Haflaga result: women who
establish a Haflaga vesset have a mean within-person cycle-length SD of 1.54 days,
compared to 3.17 days for women who establish no vesset. This confirms that Haflaga
selects the most physiologically regular subgroup of women. Figure~\ref{fig:dist}
shows the cycle length distribution and per-woman variability.

\subsection{Pattern Detection}

Pattern counting was performed within each woman's sequence independently; sequences
were never merged across women. Patient boundary pairs $(s_i, f_i)$ were encoded
explicitly and all counting loops were constrained to $[s_i, f_i]$. Extended runs of
the same pattern were counted as a single occurrence to avoid double-counting (e.g.,
four consecutive equal haflagot constitute one Haflaga, not two). All five functions
were validated against manually annotated examples and are available at
\url{https://github.com/dvirross/VessetVectorTest}.

\subsection{Null Models}
\label{sec:nullmodels}

Two null models were implemented, each run for $N = 50{,}000$ iterations with random
seed 17. Both preserve the number of cycles per woman exactly.

\paragraph{Permutation test.}
At each iteration, the $n = 1{,}554$ haflaga values are randomly permuted without
replacement. This preserves the exact empirical marginal distribution and is
equivalent to assuming all $n!$ orderings are equally probable under $H_0$
\cite{edgington2007}.

\paragraph{Multinomial null.}
At each iteration, for each woman $i$ with $n_i$ cycles, a fresh sequence
$\tilde{H}_{i,1}, \ldots, \tilde{H}_{i,n_i} \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \hat{F}$
is drawn, where $\hat{F}$ is the empirical CDF of all 1,554 observed haflaga values.
This null directly encodes the hypothesis that cycles are \textit{independent} across
time within each woman \cite{ross2022phd}. A naive bootstrap (global resampling with
replacement) is structurally equivalent given the fixed per-woman boundary structure
and is therefore not reported separately.

\subsection{Statistical Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

For each pattern $j$ and null model $m$ we computed: the observed count $O_j$; the
null mean $\bar{S}_j^{(m)}$ and SD $\sigma_j^{(m)}$; the z-score $z_j^{(m)} =
(O_j - \bar{S}_j^{(m)}) / \sigma_j^{(m)}$; and the one-sided empirical p-value
$p_j^{(m)} = \widehat{\Pr}(S_j^{(m)} \geq O_j)$.

The \textbf{joint multivariate test} uses the Euclidean distance of the observed
count vector from the null mean vector as the test statistic:
\begin{equation}
  D^{(m)} = \bigl\|\mathbf{O} - \bar{\mathbf{S}}^{(m)}\bigr\|_2
  = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{5}\bigl(O_j - \bar{S}_j^{(m)}\bigr)^2},
  \label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}
with p-value equal to the fraction of iterations where the simulated distance exceeded
$D^{(m)}$. Unlike Bonferroni correction --- which assumes independence --- this test
accommodates dependence among the five counts by operating on the joint vector
simultaneously. Inspired by King et al.\ \cite{king2018} but using distance rather
than density as the test statistic, this approach is introduced in \cite{ross2022phd};
a formal theoretical treatment is planned in future work.

A \textbf{focused joint test} restricted to the three mathematically dependent
patterns (Haflaga, Week-Dilug, Dilug-in-Dilug) is reported in Section~\ref{sec:chisq}.

Pairwise \textbf{chi-square tests of independence} between pattern types were computed
on the per-woman binary matrix (1 = woman established the pattern at least once).
$\alpha = .05$ throughout.

automatically constituting a Week-Dilug event (set-theoretic inclusion). Of the 27
Dilug-in-Dilug sequences, 16 had second difference $|d| = 1$ (near-Haflaga).

The permutation and multinomial null models are in close agreement across all five

empirical CDFs, confirming the two nulls produce nearly identical distributions for
each pattern. This robustness is expected: for a well-behaved empirical distribution,
the two nulls converge as $n \to \infty$ under regularity conditions
\cite{edgington2007}.

% ─── 5. Discussion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

This study provides the first formal statistical test of whether halachic vesset
patterns are overrepresented in empirical menstrual cycle data. Using $N = 50{,}000$
iterations of permutation and multinomial null models, we found strong and consistent
evidence for two of the five patterns analysed: \textit{Haflaga} ($z = 4.51$,
$p < .001$) and \textit{Dilug} ($z = 1.83$, $p = .043$). The joint multivariate test
confirmed collective overrepresentation ($p < .01$) and the focused test on the
mathematically dependent sub-vector was similarly significant ($p < .01$).

\subsection{Haflaga}

The Haflaga finding is the most striking result. With only 2 of 50,000 permutation
simulations reaching or exceeding the observed count of 26 (the simulation ceiling),
the empirical evidence is essentially unambiguous. The result is biologically
interpretable: Haflaga detects exactly the pattern expected in women with high
within-person cycle regularity. The present data confirm this: women who establish
Haflaga have within-person SDs of 1.54 days vs.\ 3.17 days for women who establish no
vesset at all. Haflaga is the halachic formalisation of a genuine physiological regularity.

\subsection{Dilug}

The Dilug finding ($p = .043$) is more modest but consistent across both null models.
Arithmetic progressions in cycle length may arise from gradual physiological trends
within a woman's cycle history, e.g., gradual lengthening or shortening associated
with hormonal change, ageing, or weight change. The significance of Dilug suggests
that such directional trends are more common than random arrangements would produce,
warranting replication in larger samples.

\subsection{Mathematical Dependence Structure as a Unifying Observation}

A distinctive feature of this study is that the observed statistical dependences between
pattern types are not post-hoc empirical discoveries but \emph{mathematical necessities}
derivable from the pattern definitions alone.  This is, to our knowledge, an observation
that has not previously been stated explicitly in the halachic or statistical literature.

\paragraph{The finite-difference hierarchy.}
The five vesset patterns are naturally unified by the language of finite differences.
Recall that the $n$-th forward difference operator $\Delta^n$ is defined recursively by
$\Delta^0 H_k = H_k$ and $\Delta^n H_k = \Delta^{n-1}H_{k+1} - \Delta^{n-1}H_k$.
Each vesset pattern corresponds to a condition on a specific order of $\Delta$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Pattern & Condition & Order \\
\midrule
Week-Dilug & $H_k = 30$ (constant at 30) & $\Delta^0 H_k = 30$ \\
Week       & $H_k = c \in \mathcal{W}$ (constant, anchored) & $\Delta^0 H_k = c$ \\
Haflaga    & $H_k = c$ (constant, any $c$) & $\Delta^0 H_k = c$ \\
Dilug      & $\Delta^1 H_k = d \neq 0$ (constant non-zero first difference) & \\
Dilug-in-Dilug & $\Delta^2 H_k = d \neq 0$ (constant non-zero second difference) & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
This ordering mirrors the classical Taylor expansion: a constant function is a
degree-0 polynomial, a linear function is degree-1, and a quadratic is degree-2.
Haflaga, Week, and Week-Dilug detect degree-0 sequences (with progressively tighter
constraints on which constant value is admissible); Dilug detects degree-1 sequences
($H_k = a + bk$, $b \neq 0$); and Dilug-in-Dilug detects degree-2 sequences
($H_k = a + bk + ck^2$, $c \neq 0$).  Crucially, a degree-2 sequence with a very
small leading coefficient $c$ is nearly linear, and a degree-1 sequence with a very
small slope $b$ is nearly constant --- so the hierarchy does not impose rigid
boundaries; rather, it describes a spectrum of regularity along which the patterns
sit at adjacent levels.

This graded structure generates three mathematically necessary relationships between
the patterns.

\paragraph{Haflaga and Week-Dilug: containment.}
Week-Dilug requires two consecutive \textit{haflagot} (pl.\ of \textit{haflaga}) both equal to 30, i.e.\
$\Delta^0 H_{k-1} = \Delta^0 H_k = 30$.  Haflaga-30 requires three consecutive
haflagot all equal to 30, i.e.\ $\Delta^0 H_{k-2} = \Delta^0 H_{k-1} = \Delta^0
H_k = 30$, which strictly contains the Week-Dilug condition.  Algebraically,
\[
  \bigl\{H_{k-2} = H_{k-1} = H_k = 30\bigr\}
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  \bigl\{H_{k-1} = H_k = 30\bigr\},
\]
so every Haflaga-30 sequence necessarily generates a Week-Dilug event at the same
position.  This is not a probabilistic statement but a deterministic logical
implication: any haflaga sequence that satisfies the Haflaga-30 condition
automatically satisfies the Week-Dilug condition at its first two elements.  In
the data, 3 of the 26 Haflaga sequences have value $H = 30$ and therefore each
count once towards the Week-Dilug total as a direct algebraic consequence.

\paragraph{Haflaga and Dilug-in-Dilug: limiting convergence.}
A Dilug-in-Dilug sequence with second difference $d$ has first differences
$\Delta^1 H_k = b,\, b+d,\, b+2d,\, \ldots$, so the haflaga values are
\[
  H_k = H_0 + bk + \tfrac{d}{2}k(k-1).
\]
As $d \to 0$, this reduces to $H_k = H_0 + bk$ (a Dilug), and as both $d \to 0$
and $b \to 0$, it reduces to $H_k = H_0$ (a Haflaga).  More precisely, for a run
of length $n$ starting at $H_0$, the maximum deviation from the constant sequence
$\mathbf{c} = H_0$ is bounded by
\[
  \|H - \mathbf{c}\|_\infty \;\leq\; |b|\,n + \tfrac{|d|}{2}\,n^2.
\]
A DiD sequence with $|d| = 1$ and a short run ($n = 4$, the minimum) therefore
deviates from the nearest constant by at most $4|b| + 8$ days --- for small $|b|$,
this is a sequence that is barely distinguishable from a Haflaga.  In the data,
16 of the 27 observed DiD sequences have $|d| = 1$, the minimum non-zero value;
for these, the haflaga values change by only 1 day per cycle relative to linear
trend.  The women who sustain a DiD pattern with $|d| = 1$ are the same women whose
cycles are regular enough to sustain a near-constant sequence across multiple cycles
--- biologically the same population as Haflaga women.  This explains the strong
positive dependence $\chi^2 = 9.99$, $p = .002$: the polynomial hierarchy places
Haflaga and DiD adjacent in a continuum of regularity, and the data show that real
women tend to cluster near the boundary between them.

\paragraph{Dilug and Week-Dilug: mutual exclusion.}
Dilug requires $\Delta^1 H_k = H_k - H_{k-1} = d \neq 0$; Week-Dilug requires
$\Delta^1 H_k = H_k - H_{k-1} = 0$.  Within any single cycle window $(k-1, k)$,
these conditions are contradictory:
\[
  \Delta^1 H_k \neq 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \Delta^1 H_k = 0
\]
cannot both hold.  A cycle pair that satisfies Week-Dilug ($\Delta^1 H = 0$) is
therefore excluded from contributing to a Dilug run ($\Delta^1 H \neq 0$), and vice
versa.  This mutual exclusion at the window level predicts a negative co-occurrence
tendency at the woman level.  Although the pair did not reach significance in our
sample ($\chi^2 = 1.47$, $p = .226$), the direction is negative (5 observed
co-occurrences vs.\ 7.3 expected), consistent with the prediction from the algebra.

\paragraph{Implication for inference.}
The three relationships derived above --- containment, limiting convergence, and
mutual exclusion --- show that the five pattern counts are \emph{structurally}
non-independent: their joint distribution is constrained by the algebra of
finite differences, not by coincidence.  Applying Bonferroni correction, which
assumes independence, would therefore be not merely conservative but logically
inconsistent with the mathematical structure of the patterns.  The joint
multivariate test of Section~\ref{sec:inference} sidesteps this problem by operating
directly on the count vector, without any independence assumption, thereby respecting
the dependence structure that the finite-difference hierarchy imposes.

\subsection{The Joint Multivariate Test}

The joint test (Eq.~\ref{eq:distance}) is an original methodological contribution
\cite{ross2022phd}. It is \textit{inspired by} the simulation-based testing framework
of King et al.\ \cite{king2018}, who propose a multivariate kernel density estimator
to find the smallest acceptance region in the joint space of test statistics. The
present approach is structurally simpler: it uses Euclidean distance from the null
mean as the test statistic, requires no density estimation, and has a transparent
geometric interpretation (the observed vector either falls inside or outside the null
cloud). A formal comparison of the two approaches --- including theoretical properties
such as consistency, power, and sensitivity to dependence among counts --- is planned
in future work.

\subsection{Non-Significant Patterns}

Week, Week-Dilug, and Dilug-in-Dilug were not individually significant. These results
do not invalidate these halachic rules, which are established on legal-theological
grounds \cite{ross2021statistical}. The non-significance of Dilug-in-Dilug is
particularly notable given its large z-score at the sequence level in the joint test:
it is the dependence with Haflaga (via the polynomial hierarchy) that elevates its
collective contribution to $D$, even when its individual count is exactly at the null
mean. This illustrates why joint and individual tests can differ.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations of the dataset and methodology are acknowledged, largely following
the systematic assessment in \cite{ross2022phd}.

\paragraph{Day-night timing (onset period).}
A central requirement of halachic vesset law is that a pattern may be established
only if all its constituent menstruations arrived during the \emph{same onset period}
--- either all during the daytime period or all during the nighttime period.  The NFP dataset records cycle length in whole days but not
the time of day of menstrual onset.  This creates two types of misclassification:
(i)~patterns that are counted as established in our analysis may not qualify
halachically because the onsets spanned both day and night; and (ii)~patterns that
are not counted may in fact qualify because the day-rounding convention used here
differs from the halachic one.  Both types of error introduce noise that attenuates
the observed pattern frequencies.  The net direction of misclassification bias is indeterminate without
data on onset timing: sequences counted as established patterns may not qualify
halachically (upward bias), while sequences that do qualify halachically may be
missed (downward bias). The two effects partially cancel, but their relative
magnitude cannot be assessed from the present data, and the limitation applies
equally to all five pattern types.

\paragraph{Calendar dates.}
The dataset does not record calendar dates, making it impossible to test the 10
date-dependent vesset types (e.g., \textit{vesset hachodesh}, the monthly calendar
anchor, and weekday-based patterns).  Only the 7 cycle-length-based vesset types
are accessible; of these, two (Dilug Chozer Chalila and Haflaga Chozer Chalila) had
zero occurrences, leaving the 5 patterns analysed here.

\paragraph{Body-sign vesset.}
The dataset does not include prodromal physical symptoms (\textit{vesset haguf}, body-sign vesset: a category established when a woman
experiences a consistent recurring physical sensation --- such as yawning,
shivering, or lower-abdominal pain --- immediately preceding menstrual onset).  Women in the sample may have established body-sign
vesset patterns that are entirely invisible to a cycle-length analysis.

\paragraph{Population generalizability.}
The participants are non-Jewish NFP users \cite{fehring2013}.  Several halachic
authorities hold that physiological regularities observed in non-Jewish women
may not generalize to Jewish women in the context of halachic practice
\cite{ross2022phd}.  More broadly, NFP users are a self-selected group who are
likely more cycle-aware and may have more regular cycles than the general population,
introducing a potential upward bias in regularity-dependent patterns such as Haflaga.

\paragraph{Null model structure.}
The permutation and multinomial null models shuffle cycle lengths globally across
women, ignoring within-person autocorrelation structure \cite{bortot2010,symul2019}.
A stratified null that preserves within-person temporal order while randomising
assignments between women would provide a more stringent test.  Future work could
also employ parametric nulls based on fitted state-space models \cite{bortot2010}.

\paragraph{Sample size and power.}
The requirement for minimum run lengths (3 cycles for Haflaga and Dilug; 4 for
Dilug-in-Dilug) limits the number of testable windows, particularly for women with
few recorded cycles.  With a mean of 13.2 cycles per woman, longer patterns such as
Dilug-in-Dilug are likely underpowered.

% ─── 6. Conclusion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

This study provides the first rigorous statistical test of whether halachic vesset
patterns are overrepresented in empirical menstrual cycle data. The five patterns
analysed form a polynomial hierarchy whose structure generates mathematically
necessary dependencies, confirmed by pairwise chi-square tests. Permutation and
multinomial null models --- run for 50,000 iterations each and in close agreement
throughout --- establish that Haflaga ($p < .001$) and Dilug ($p = .043$) are
significantly overrepresented, while a joint multivariate test confirms collective
overrepresentation of all five patterns ($p < .01$). The Haflaga result is
particularly compelling: only 2 of 50,000 permutation simulations reached the observed
count. A focused joint test on the three mathematically dependent patterns
(Haflaga, Week-Dilug, Dilug-in-Dilug) is similarly significant ($p < .01$). These
findings provide empirical statistical foundations for two of the most frequently
applied halachic vesset rules, and introduce a joint distance-based test for dependent
count vectors as a methodological contribution with potential applications beyond
the halachic context.
