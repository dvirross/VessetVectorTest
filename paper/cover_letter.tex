\medskip

I am pleased to submit the manuscript \textbf{``Null-Model Choice for Rule-Defined
Pattern Counts in Clustered Longitudinal Sequences: A Case Study of Jewish Halachic
Menstrual Anticipation Rules''} for consideration as a \textbf{Case Study} in
\textit{Biometrical Journal}.

\medskip
\textbf{The problem.}
Jewish religious law prescribes that a woman anticipate her next menstruation from
recurring arithmetic patterns in her cycle history (the \textit{vesset} rules). Each rule
defines an exact event --- three equal consecutive intervals, a constant first or second
difference, a repeated weekly anchor --- and the natural question is whether such events
occur in real cycle data at a frequency different from what a suitable notion of chance
predicts. The
statistical difficulty is that the answer depends entirely on what ``chance'' is taken
to mean: the data are 1,554 cycles from 118 women (5--45 cycles each) from the publicly
archived Marquette University dataset (Fehring, 2012), collected in the randomised trial
of Fehring et al.\ (\textit{Contraception}, 2013); the pattern counts are integer-valued, structurally
dependent by definition (one rule's event set is contained in another's), and both
between-woman heterogeneity in regularity and within-woman ordering can generate them.

\medskip
\textbf{What the paper does.}
We compare three exchangeability nulls for the same five-count vector: a global
permutation null (cycle lengths exchangeable across women), a multinomial i.i.d.\ null,
and a within-woman permutation null that conditions on each woman's observed multiset of
cycle lengths and randomises only their order. All three are analysed with the same
marginal Monte Carlo tests and a joint quadratic-form (Mahalanobis) permutation statistic
whose covariance is estimated from the null replicates. The two global nulls reject
strongly (joint $p < .001$); the within-woman null does not (joint $p = .11$), and all
five observed counts fall at or below their within-woman null means. The paper shows
why this contrast arises and what each null does and does not test. A compact
simulation study reports the empirical size of every test used (including a split-batch
check on the covariance estimate), the power of the within-woman tests against
autoregressive and exact-repetition alternatives, and the sensitivity of the
conclusions to unequal follow-up (truncation and leave-one-woman-out). The contrast
reproduces in an independent, openly deposited Creighton Model cohort of 270 women and
2,139 cycles analysed with the same code, seed and simulation sizes (global $\DM = 10.04$; within-woman
$\DM = 1.22$, $p = .92$). An exploratory between-woman pairwise analysis is provided as
Supporting Information.

\medskip
\textbf{Why a Case Study.}
The statistical tools are established --- conditional randomisation, exchangeability
blocks, quadratic-form permutation statistics --- and the manuscript does not claim a
new method. Its contribution is the careful construction and contrast of scientifically
distinct conditional nulls for rule-defined pattern counts in clustered longitudinal
sequences, the demonstration that the same data give diametrically different answers
under population-level and within-subject exchangeability, and the validation of the
procedures by simulation. The lesson transfers to other settings in which deterministic
rules are applied to recurrent-event sequences (for example, exact inter-seizure or
inter-episode intervals), and this is the first formal randomisation-based test of the
halachic rules on empirical data of which we are aware.
